# HR, Tax, Insurance & Payroll Guide for WTFManuals.com

## Disclaimer
This guide provides general information to help founders think about HR, tax, insurance and payroll considerations. It is **not** legal or financial advice. Consult qualified professionals in your jurisdiction.

## 1. Business Structure & Registration
- Decide whether to form an LLC, C‑Corporation, or other entity type. An LLC offers flexibility and pass‑through taxation; a C‑Corp may be preferable for venture funding.  
- Register your business with your state and obtain an Employer Identification Number (EIN).  
- Register the domain "WTFManuals.com" and any trademarks or copyrights associated with your brand.

## 2. Tax Considerations
- **Federal & State Taxes:** Understand applicable corporate income taxes, sales taxes, and franchise taxes.  
- **Sales Tax Collection:** If you sell subscriptions, determine if digital services are taxable in customer states/countries. Tools like Avalara or Stripe Tax can help automate collection.  
- **Quarterly Estimated Taxes:** Pay quarterly taxes if you expect to owe more than a certain amount.  
- **R&D Credits:** Research tax credits for software development; consult a tax advisor for eligibility.

## 3. Hiring & Payroll
- **Classify Workers:** Determine whether team members are employees or independent contractors. Misclassification can lead to penalties.  
- **On‑boarding:** Collect Form W‑4 or W‑9, state withholding forms, I‑9 eligibility verification and direct deposit information.  
- **Payroll Providers:** Consider using Gusto, Rippling, or ADP to automate payroll, tax filings and benefits. For early stages, Gusto’s base package is affordable and integrates with accounting software.  
- **Freelancer Platforms:** Use Upwork or Freelancer for short‑term contracts; ensure contract language includes IP assignment.  
- **Compensation:** Set salaries based on market rates; offer equity through stock option plans—consult legal counsel on 409A valuations.

## 4. Insurance
- **General Liability:** Covers bodily injury and property damage claims (e.g., if a user claims harm due to following a manual).  
- **Professional Liability (Errors & Omissions):** Covers claims of negligence related to your software and manuals.  
- **Cyber Liability:** Protects against data breaches and privacy lawsuits.  
- **Workers’ Compensation:** Mandatory for employees in most states.  
- **Health Insurance:** Offer group health plans or stipends as you grow; use PEO services to access affordable plans.

## 5. Record Keeping
- Keep detailed records of expenses, invoices, payroll, taxes and insurance policies. Use accounting software like Wave (free for small businesses) or QuickBooks.  
- Maintain HR files for each employee, including signed contracts, performance reviews and benefit elections.  
- Use a secure document management system (e.g., Notion or Dropbox) with controlled access.

## 6. Compliance & Best Practices
- Comply with labour laws, including minimum wage, overtime, anti‑discrimination and mandatory poster requirements.  
- Ensure privacy compliance (GDPR, CCPA) by implementing strong data protection policies.  
- Train employees on cybersecurity (phishing awareness) and safety procedures.  
- Conduct annual policy reviews and update handbooks accordingly.

## 7. Founder Tips
- Hire a payroll accountant or use a PEO early to avoid compliance surprises.  
- Invest in employee handbooks and clear job descriptions; they improve expectations and performance.  
- Keep personal and business finances separate; open a dedicated business bank account.  
- Reassess insurance coverage annually as the business scales.
